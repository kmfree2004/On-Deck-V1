About On Deck

Helping players find the future that fits them

On Deck helps young softball players see everything softball can lead to and find the future that is right for them. That might be playing in college, going pro with the AUSL, coaching, training or healing athletes, working in sports media, or playing for the love of it.

We share the real paths, honest numbers and people who have walked them, so players can choose with good information. Where a player lands is her decision, and "softball is just for fun" is a good answer.

We started by asking players. Former players told us they wished they had known more about their options, and most said pro softball did not feel realistic. Almost all said they played for the love of the game.

Real numbers

The honest version of pro softball

Players told us pro softball did not feel realistic or did not pay enough. Hype does not help with either. Here is what is public today, and what we still need to confirm with the league.

What is public today

  • 6 teamsBandits, Blaze, Talons, Volts, Spark and Cascade. Spark and Cascade joined as expansion teams for 2026.
  • 25 gamesEach team played 25 games from June 9 to July 20, 2026.
  • 17 ticketsThe May 4, 2026 College Draft placed 17 players who received a "Golden Ticket."
  • On ESPNESPN is a broadcast partner, and Major League Baseball made a strategic investment in the league.
  • 98%of NCAA student-athletes go pro in something other than sports, according to the NCAA.

Still to confirm with the AUSL

  • Pay and benefits for players, by season and role
  • Roster size and how many new spots open each year
  • Softball-specific odds of moving from high school to college to pro
  • How the Reserve Athlete Pool is chosen
  • How players outside college can opt in
  • What a player's year looks like outside June and July
  • Guidelines for youth events and for contact with minors

We will publish these only after the league confirms them.

Way in

College Draft

College players who receive a Golden Ticket are drafted each spring and assigned to a team.

Way in

Allocation Draft

Players who opt in can be chosen, including players from the Reserve Athlete Pool and from other pro leagues.

Way in

Expansion Draft

New teams can select players the original teams did not protect. This only happens when the league adds teams.

    Recruiting basics

    How to get seen

    How recruiting usually works
    • Build a list of schools, then narrow it by level, location, academics and cost.
    • If you plan to play at the NCAA Division I or II level, register with the NCAA Eligibility Center and keep your grades on track.
    • Contact coaches yourself with a short email and a skills video.
    • Go to camps, showcases and campus visits at schools you really like.
    • Compare offers, ask questions, and decide with your family.
    Ways to get seen
    • Make a short skills video with a clear view of you, and update it each year.
    • Pick showcases that coaches from your target schools attend.
    • Ask your club or high school coach to reach out to programs for you.
    • Keep your stats, schedule and grades current and easy to share.
    What to put in an email to a coach

    Use the Email a coach tab for templates, and the email builder in My plan to fill them in for you.

    Questions to ask a program
    • How many players at my position are on the roster, and what role would I likely have?
    • What does a typical week look like?
    • How does the program support academics and injuries?
    • What happens to my scholarship if I get hurt?

    NCAA rules limit when coaches can start contacting recruits and how they can reply. The dates differ by division and change over time, so check the current NCAA recruiting calendar for your class year and ask your coach before you act.

    Email a coach

    What you are hoping to get from an email

    An email is a short introduction. You are not asking for an offer. You are hoping to:

    • Get on a coach's radar for your graduation year and position.
    • Find out whether the program is recruiting your class and position.
    • Get feedback on your video.
    • Learn where and when the coach will see you play.
    • Show real interest in the program, which coaches notice.

    Many first emails get no reply, and that is normal. Coaches get a lot of messages, and NCAA rules can limit how and when they reply. It is not a no. Wait three or four weeks, then send a short update with something new, such as a new video or schedule.

    Camps and showcases

    What you are hoping to get from them

    • Be seen by coaches who recruit your position and graduation year.
    • Get honest feedback on where you stand compared with the level you are aiming for.
    • See the campus, the facilities and how the coaches teach.
    • Meet current players and ask what the program is really like.
    • Find out whether it feels like a fit. A camp is a test for you too.

    A camp invite, or a good day at camp, does not mean a coach plans to offer you a spot. Ask directly: "Where do I stand with your program?"

    Four kinds of events

    College camps (run by one school)

    Good when the school is on your list. You get instruction and a look from the coaches you want to see you. They usually cost money, so save them for schools you are really interested in.

    Multi-school camps

    Coaches from several schools take part, so one event can put you in front of several programs. Check ahead of time which schools will be there.

    Showcases

    A club or organization invites college coaches to watch players. They are for showing what you can already do, not for learning. Ask the organizer or your coach which college coaches are expected.

    Skills clinics

    Short sessions focused on a skill like pitching, hitting or catching. Take them to get better, not to be recruited.

    How to choose which ones to attend

    1. Start with your list of schools.
    2. Check each school's softball website for a camps page. Note the dates, ages and cost.
    3. Ask your club or high school coach which events coaches from your list attend.
    4. Check your tournament schedule so nothing conflicts.
    5. Email the coach to ask the questions below before you pay.
    6. Choose a few events that fit, not all of them.

    You do not need to attend many. Not every family can afford several camps, and a few well-chosen events are better than a long list.

    Questions to ask before you register

    • Will the coaching staff evaluate players at this camp?
    • Which session fits my position and graduation year?
    • How many players attend each session?
    • What does the fee cover? Are there lower-cost options?

    Rate a camp before you sign up

    • The school is on my list.
    • The staff who recruit my position will be there.
    • I will be evaluated, not only coached.
    • It does not conflict with my club schedule.
    • My family can afford it, including travel.

    If most of these are true, the camp is worth a serious look.

    Before, during and after

    Before
    • Send a camp question email using the template.
    • Have your video link and schedule ready.
    • Know your position, your strengths and one thing you are working on.
    During
    • Introduce yourself to the coaches and say what school you are interested in.
    • Show you are coachable: listen, try the adjustment and ask questions.
    • Meet a current player and ask what a normal week looks like.
    After
    • Send a thank-you email within a few days using the After a camp template.
    • Ask for feedback and what the coach would like to see next.
    • Add the camp and the date to your tracker.

    Source for event types and choosing advice: Extra Inning Softball, summer camps, clinics and showcases. Which events matter most varies by region and by year, so ask your coach for a short list.

      Coach toolkit

      Help players see the whole map

      Coaches are the main source of guidance. The coaches we heard from already talk about careers outside playing. This toolkit helps them describe pro softball accurately alongside everything else.

      Start the conversation
      • "What do you love most about softball?"
      • "What would you want softball to be in your life in ten years?"
      • "What would you like to know that nobody has told you yet?"
      Describe pro softball accurately

      Say that the AUSL is a real league with limited spots, a short season, and public ways in. Share the numbers from the page above and avoid both hype and dismissal. Let the player decide how much it matters to them.

      Make room for "I just want to play"

      Some players want softball in their lives without wanting it as a career. Treat that as a complete answer. Point them to college, club, adult leagues or coaching.

      Be patient with players who know their options but are not ready

      Some players understand what is out there and still do not want to explore it yet. Check in again later instead of pressing, and let them know the door stays open. Staying in the sports world does not require being the athlete.

      Show the paths beside the field

      Training, rehab, media and coaching are all softball careers. Invite a former player or a local professional to talk to the team for fifteen minutes.
